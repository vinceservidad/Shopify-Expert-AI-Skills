# Copy Quality Check

Run this check on every piece of copy before handoff: product pages, listings, ads, emails, SMS, support replies, SEO text, and client updates. Run it as a separate pass after the draft is written. It sits on top of the skill's truth, claim, and policy checks and never replaces them.

## Fix each of these

1. **"It's not X, it's Y."** Just say Y.
2. **Question hooks and rhetorical setups** ("What if I told you", "Think about it:", "Plot twist:"). Lead with the answer, the result, or the number.
3. **Buzzwords** (game-changer, unlock, leverage, elevate, delve, robust, streamline, supercharge, harness, transformative). Use plain words. Keep a word when it is the exact platform term.
4. **Weak endings** ("What do you think?", mic-drop closing lines, recaps that start with "Overall" or "In conclusion"). End on the point.
5. **Bullets always in threes.** Use as many bullets as the point needs.
6. **Vague claims.** Use real figures from the brief, the store data, or a named source. If there are none, cut the claim or mark the gap, for example `[NEEDS OWNER: return rate source]`.
7. **Colon reveals** ("The best part: it learns."). Write a plain sentence. Use colons only for lists, labels, and quotes.
8. **Trailing "-ing" clauses that claim meaning** (highlighting, showcasing, underscoring, reflecting). Cut them or state the fact.
9. **Weasel attribution** ("experts agree", "studies show"). Name the source or cut the claim. Never invent a source.
10. **Synonym cycling** (agent, then assistant, then tool for the same thing). Repeat the clear word. For metrics, use the platform's name every time, such as `Purchases`, `Conversions`, or `Sessions`.
11. **Negative listing** ("Not a X. Not a Y. A Z."). State Z.
12. **Dramatic fragments and robotic rhythm** ("That's it. That's the whole thing."). Write full sentences and vary their length.
13. **Em dashes.** None in short text (messages, posts, ad copy, SMS, subject lines). At most 2 in long text.
14. **Formatting slop.** No emoji in headers and no mid-sentence bold.
15. **Portability test.** If a line could fit any other person, client, or product unchanged, make it specific or cut it.

## Examples

| Before | After |
| --- | --- |
| It's not just a mug, it's a morning ritual. | Holds 16 oz and keeps coffee hot for 6 hours. [source: spec sheet] |
| Tired of tangled cords? | The 2 m braided cable does not tangle in a bag. |
| Studies show customers love fast shipping. | Orders ship within 1 business day. [source: shipping policy] |
| The best part: it's machine washable. | It is machine washable on a cold cycle. |
| We've shipped your order, showcasing our commitment to speed. | Your order shipped on [verified date]. |

The examples are synthetic. Each fact in the After column needs a real source before use.

## Editing a merchant's or client's draft

- Keep their voice: their hedges, bluntness, asides, and long spoken sentences.
- Make the smallest edit that fixes each problem. Do not rewrite lines that pass.
- Show the edited draft first, then a short "What changed" list with one line per edit.

## Limits

- A brand voice guide from the merchant wins over this list when they conflict. Note the conflict in the handoff.
- Exact customer quotations stay verbatim. Label them as quotations and do not edit them to pass this check.
- Passing this check does not prove the copy is true, compliant, or effective.
