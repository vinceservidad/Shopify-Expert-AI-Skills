## Rule of One

Choose one primary:

- reader
- problem or job
- promise
- proof path
- offer
- action

Secondary information can support the decision, but it should not compete with the central message.

## Awareness and intent

Use awareness stages as a message-planning framework, not as proof about the visitor. Infer stage only from supplied traffic, query, creative, research, or page context.

- Problem-aware traffic needs the problem framed accurately.
- Solution-aware traffic needs category and mechanism clarity.
- Product-aware traffic needs differentiation, proof, fit, and risk reduction.
- Most-aware traffic needs offer, availability, and low-friction commitment.


Remove or soften a claim when proof does not support its precision or certainty.

## Page sequence

A product page can include:

1. identity, image, price, variant, and purchase control
2. concise outcome or use-case statement
3. proof closest to the claim it supports
4. key benefits grounded in features or evidence
5. use, fit, specifications, ingredients, or materials
6. comparison or selection guidance with a defined basis
7. shipping, returns, warranty, subscription, and guarantee truth
8. objections and frequently asked questions
9. relevant recommendation, bundle, or replenishment path

The exact order should follow the customer's decision and mobile context. Do not add sections only to make the page longer.

## Copy principles

- lead with the clearest useful benefit supported by facts
- prefer specific nouns and active verbs
- translate features into benefits without exaggerating outcomes
- place qualifications where a customer can see them
- avoid fake scarcity, fake social proof, and unsupported superlatives
- preserve search-relevant product language where it helps discovery and comprehension
- use short paragraphs and scannable labels without reducing important policy information
