# FTC Endorsement and Disclosure Compliance for Creative

Apply these verified US Federal Trade Commission rules when briefing, scripting, or reviewing UGC, creator ads, testimonials, and review-led creative. Confirm non-US market advertising rules with local counsel when running international campaigns.

## Official references

- <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-255> (FTC Endorsement Guides, 16 CFR Part 255)
- <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/part-465> (FTC Trade Regulation Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials, 16 CFR Part 465)
- <https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing/endorsements-influencers-reviews> (FTC Endorsements, Influencers, and Reviews guidance)
- <https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/plain-language/1001a-influencer-guide-508_1.pdf> (FTC Disclosures 101 for Social Media Influencers)

## Material connections and disclosure rules (16 CFR Part 255)

When an endorser has a connection to the brand that viewers would not expect, the creative must disclose it clearly and conspicuously.

- **What requires disclosure:** Cash payment, free or discounted products, affiliate commissions, or employment and family ties all create material connections.
- **Format parity:** Put the disclosure in the same medium as the claim. For video UGC and Reels or TikTok ads, place a legible visual disclosure on screen and speak it aloud if the endorsement is spoken. Do not bury disclosures below a "more" toggle or inside a wall of hashtags.
- **Safe-zone visibility:** Keep visual disclosures inside placement safe zones in high-contrast text long enough for viewers to read. Do not rely solely on a platform's built-in paid partnership tag if viewers can easily miss it.
- **Plain wording:** Use direct phrases such as `Ad`, `Paid partnership with [Brand]`, or `Sponsored`. Avoid ambiguous abbreviations like `#sp`, `#collab`, or `#ambassador` on their own.

## Honest experience and claim substantiation

- **Actual use requirement:** An endorser claiming personal use must have actually used the product and hold the stated opinion. Never script a paid actor to claim "my honest unpaid review" or disguise studio ads as spontaneous customer clips.
- **Claim substantiation parity:** Brands cannot make health, performance, or efficacy claims through a creator or customer quote that the brand cannot substantiate directly with competent evidence.
- **Typicality for results claims:** If a testimonial or before-and-after creative shows an exceptional outcome, either hold substantiation that the result is typical or clearly and conspicuously disclose what consumers can generally expect. A generic "results not typical" disclaimer does not satisfy Part 255.

## Prohibitions carrying civil penalty risk (16 CFR Part 465)

Under the FTC Trade Regulation Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials, the agency can seek civil penalties for knowing violations.

- **Fake reviews and testimonials:** Do not create, buy, or feature fake customer reviews, fabricated testimonials, or AI-generated personas claiming real product experience.
- **Undisclosed insider endorsements:** Officers, employees, or agency staff appearing in UGC or testimonials must clearly disclose their relationship to the business.
- **Incentivized positive reviews and fake influence:** Never condition payment, discounts, or gifts on writing a positive review. Do not buy fake followers, views, or engagement signals to inflate a creator's credibility.

## Creative QA checklist before release

1. Verify signed usage rights and confirm whether the presenter is a paid creator, gifted partner, customer, or company insider.
2. Confirm every material connection has a clear visual and, for spoken video claims, audible disclosure inside placement safe zones.
3. Map every product and outcome claim in the script to verified brand substantiation, and add generally expected results if a featured outcome is atypical.
4. Remove any fabricated customer quotes, unverified star ratings, or misleading "unpaid review" framing before handoff to media buying.
