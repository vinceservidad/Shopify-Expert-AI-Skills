# Hook Development

Hooks earn attention by making a relevant promise, tension, question, demonstration, or proof legible quickly. They do not need sensationalism.

## Evidence sources

- exact customer quotations with source and permission
- search queries and on-site search terms
- support and returns themes
- product differentiators and demonstrations
- common objections and comparison criteria
- verified performance patterns within a named scope

Do not turn analyst language into a customer quotation.

## Hook families

Use these as generation frameworks:

- **Problem recognition:** name a specific situation the audience already experiences.
- **Outcome orientation:** state an approved desired outcome without promising certainty.
- **Demonstration:** show the product, mechanism, fit, or use in context.
- **Objection:** address a sourced reason not to buy.
- **Comparison:** clarify a real selection difference using a fair basis.
- **Proof:** lead with verifiable evidence and its scope.
- **Curiosity:** create a relevant information gap that the asset actually resolves.
- **Identity or job:** connect the product to a supported role, value, or task.

## Quality checks

- understandable without audio when the placement requires it
- aligned with the actual landing experience and offer
- resolved by the body of the creative
- supported by product truth and proof
- distinct enough to answer a learning question
- usable within placement safe zones and accessibility needs
- free of fabricated urgency, shame, impersonation, or unsupported transformation
